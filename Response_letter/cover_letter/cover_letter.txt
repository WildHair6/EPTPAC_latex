Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript entitled "Exact Prescribed-Time
Prescribed-Accuracy Control for Spacecraft under Dual Reaction Wheel
Saturation" for consideration in the Journal of Guidance, Control, and
Dynamics.

Modern spacecraft missions, such as on-orbit rendezvous, autonomous
docking, and multi-vehicle formation reconfiguration, impose strict
requirements on both the convergence time and the pointing accuracy of
attitude maneuvers. For reaction-wheel-actuated spacecraft, meeting
these requirements is further complicated by two coupled physical
constraints that must be respected simultaneously: torque saturation,
which limits angular acceleration, and angular-momentum saturation,
which caps the attainable angular velocity. Existing prescribed-time
controllers either neglect the momentum constraint or converge well
before the specified deadline, degrading mission efficiency and
potentially disrupting inter-vehicle coordination. This paper presents
a two-loop framework in which an outer-loop planner generates a
reference trajectory that exactly reaches the target attitude at the
user-specified terminal time while strictly satisfying both saturation
limits, and an inner-loop nonsingular controller drives the tracking
error to within a prescribed accuracy bound despite inertia
uncertainties and external disturbances. A parameter-synthesis
procedure coordinates the two loops and establishes the minimum
feasible terminal time. Numerical simulations validate the exact
terminal-time convergence, prescribed tracking accuracy, and
dual-saturation compliance.

We believe this work may be of interest to the JGCD readership working
on spacecraft attitude guidance and control.

We confirm that this manuscript is original, is not currently under
review elsewhere, and has been approved by all authors. There are no
conflicts of interest to declare.
